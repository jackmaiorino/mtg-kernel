A new top bot, a search miss, and Vintage Cube's first run

Basilisk 3 reached first place on the public Pauper leaderboard at 1,619.5 Elo. Meanwhile, lookahead search fell short in a decision screen, and our Vintage Cube trainer finished its first shakedown.

New here? MTG Kernel is an open-source project teaching a computer to play Magic: The Gathering from scratch. It has never watched a human play. The bot learns by playing itself and earlier bots. Its only reward comes from winning. Each week we share one chart about what it learned.

Basilisk 3 takes first place

Last week we asked whether the Pauper bot would keep improving past 1.59 million training steps. On those three runs, reaching 2.1 million steps added another 3.8 percentage points against Basilisk 1 over the 1.05-million-step mark. Since then, a newer three-run line took over: it also learns whether to throw back a weak opening hand (a mulligan) and when to act during the opponent's turn (action windows). After about 15.7 million total games past its fork, that line gained 9.3 percentage points against its fork starting point, and using its own mulligans beat always keeping seven cards by 6.7 percentage points.

A frozen checkpoint from that line, named Basilisk 3, entered the public Spellbench Pauper leaderboard on November 26. Across 384 seat-swapped rated games (292 wins, 92 losses, zero draws or engine halts), it ranked first of 10 bots at 1,619.5 Elo (95% range 1,561.1 to 1,681.0), 71.9 Elo ahead of Basilisk 2 at 1,547.6 Elo. In head-to-head matches of 128 games each, Basilisk 3 won 72.7% against Basilisk 1, 78.1% against Basilisk 1 picking its single top-ranked move (argmax), and 77.3% against Archon 3. One caveat: Basilisk 3 trained with mulligans and action windows, while this leaderboard plays without either.

Why lookahead search missed

We also tested whether looking ahead (tree search guided by the bot's value head, which estimates the chance of winning from a board state) improves Basilisk 3's choices. In a countersigned 900-decision screen, no search setting passed the +0.8-point bar to advance. The primary search arm scored -0.49 percentage points against plain policy play (95% range -1.04 to +0.06), and a faster value-only arm scored -2.07 points. Even flat rollouts with no value head reached only +0.23 points (95% range -0.34 to +0.84). This is a negative decision-panel result on one set of positions, not a full-game strength test; a separate full-game search test is still running.

Follow-up diagnostics (every number here is exploratory and does not change the screen verdict) pointed to two problems. First, hidden cards hurt lookahead: across 900 decisions at 16 rollouts per move, an exploratory chooser shown the true hidden cards gained +2.00 percentage points, while the same chooser sampling possible opponent hands scored -0.06 points, losing more than half of the true-state gain (at 64 rollouts it reached +0.31 points). Second, on 420 eligible decisions, the value head's exploratory ranking score was 0.206 against an implied ceiling of 0.744, and its training target did not exactly match how the screen graded game continuations.

Vintage Cube's first shakedown run

This week we also shifted our main engineering push toward Vintage Cube, where eight players draft from a shared pool of 540 iconic Magic cards, build 40-card decks, and play. Before training a real Cube player, we ran a full pipeline shakedown where the deck builder and drafter learn while games themselves are played by a random legal-move pilot. The first try stopped cleanly at update 14 after 16,800 games when a halt-rate tripwire caught five rules bugs in one 2,000-game window. After fixing those interactions (such as free X-cost spells, library shuffles during a search, and self-attaching Equipment) across 137 pull requests merged to main since November 20, the restarted shakedown completed all 128 builder-only updates (143,360 games, 0.045% first-attempt halts) and all 128 draft-and-build updates (143,360 games, 0.031% halts) with zero stops.

Across four countersigned shakedown reads under random play (2,048 games per check, 8,190 of 8,192 games completed), the deck builder climbed from +4.33 percentage points at update 32 to +17.72 at update 64, +23.83 at update 96, and +24.37 points at update 128 (95% range +19.87% to +29.05%) over its untrained starting weights, settling near 23 non-land cards in a 40-card deck. These are shakedown reads under random play, not playing strength, and the shakedown weights are discarded after the report. Along the way, two byte-identical speedups accelerated Cube training without changing a single training outcome: typed in-process game transport ran 1.47x faster (mean update time down from 171.04 s to 116.49 s), and sharing idle game slots across adjacent draft pods added another 1.078x speedup (130.50 s to 121.02 s).
