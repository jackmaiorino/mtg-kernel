A bot that taught itself Magic is still getting better

Every time we double its practice, it wins more games. Just not as many more as it used to.

New here? MTG Kernel is an open-source project teaching a computer to play Magic: The Gathering from scratch. It has never seen a human play. It learns only by playing games against itself, and the only feedback it ever gets is whether it won. Each week we share one chart about what it learned.

What we learned

The bot plays Pauper, a style of Magic where only the most common cards are allowed. To measure progress, we pit each new version against the one it started from (Basilisk 1, which is public). A 71% win rate means today's bot beats its old self in 71 of every 100 games.

Early on, each doubling of practice added 3 to 4.5 points of win rate. Past a million training steps, it looks closer to 2.5 points. It is definitely still improving. Whether it is really slowing down is not certain yet: the data cannot rule out the old pace.

So besides training longer, we are handing it decisions it used to skip: whether to throw back a bad opening hand (a mulligan), and when to respond during the opponent's turn. It will have to work out both on its own.

Next week

The games at twice the practice are already played, and we open the results next week. If it is still climbing at the same rate, expect about 72%. If it has stalled, about 70%.
