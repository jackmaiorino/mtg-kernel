What we learned

Our main training chains learn Pauper by playing against themselves and a few earlier models, and the only reward is winning. We test them against Basilisk 1, the public model they started from. Basilisk 1 scored 49.79% against itself on the same deals. At 1.59 million updates, the three chains averaged 70.76% against it, a gain of 20.96 percentage points. A draw counts as half a win.

The last doubling before 1.05 million updates gained 3.34 points. Across the later checkpoints, the estimated gain was 2.44 points per doubling. Its 95% interval, 1.41 to 3.46, includes that earlier pace. Comparing just the first and last of those checkpoints gives a gain of 1.08 points, with an interval that includes zero.

That shapes how we spend the fleet. More of the same training still pays, probably more slowly. So the next experiments add what the bot could not do before: mulligans and full priority windows are next to go into training, as their own forks.

Next week

At the time of this analysis, two of the three chains had played their 2.1M games. Their results were still sealed. Projecting from 1.05M gives a gain over Basilisk 1 of about 22 points if the later trend continues, or about 20 points if it stays flat.
