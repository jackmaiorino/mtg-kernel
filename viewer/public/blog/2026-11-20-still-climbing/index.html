A bot that taught itself Magic is still getting better

After 1.59 million training steps, the bot won about 71 in every 100 games against its starting version. How much did the latest practice help?

New here? MTG Kernel is an open-source project teaching a computer to play Magic: The Gathering from scratch. It has never watched a human play. The bot learns by playing itself and earlier bots. Its only reward comes from winning. Each week we share one chart about what it learned.

What we learned

The bot plays Pauper, a version of Magic that allows only cards printed at its most common rarity. We test it against Basilisk 1, the public bot it started from. Basilisk 1 won 1,936 of 3,888 games against a copy of itself (49.79%). On the same deals, the three trained versions won 8,253 of 11,664 games (70.76%). Training gained about 21 wins per 100 games.

One training step is one small adjustment to the bot after a batch of 10 practice games, so 1.59 million steps means about 16 million games. Through the first million training steps, doubling practice added about 2.6 to 4.5 wins per 100 games. Across the later tests, another doubling of practice was worth an estimated 2.4 extra wins per 100 games. The latest pace looks slower, though the earlier pace still fits the data.

So besides training longer, we are handing it decisions it used to skip: whether to throw back a bad opening hand (a mulligan), and when to respond during the opponent's turn. It will have to work out both on its own.

Next week

At the time of this analysis, two of the three training runs had played their 2.1-million-step test games. We hadn't opened their results yet. Starting from the million-step score, the later pace would put the bot near 72% at 2.1 million steps. A flat result would put it near 70%.

The four early doublings gained 2.58, 4.51, 3.95 and 3.34 percentage points. Across nine later tests per run, the estimate was 2.44 points per doubling (95% range 1.41 to 3.46). Comparing just the 1.05M and 1.59M endpoints gives a gain of 1.08 points (95% range −0.13 to 2.21).

All of these test games ended in a win or loss. We track performance against other opponents separately.
