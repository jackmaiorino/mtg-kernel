# Deck model: sideboarding now, brewing later: design v1

Date: 2026-09-09. Author: LEAD (Fable) with a four-design judge panel. Revision 3 (after Claude panel review rounds 1 and 2 standing in for the Codex consult; all findings adopted). Status: DRAFT for Jack's rulings (section 10).

## 1. Goal and claims kept separate

Goal: the shortest verified path to data-driven, disclosed sideboard tables for MTGO Leagues using the current frozen checkpoint, then a separate deck model that amortizes and refines that table. This document starts from the judge panel's winning design (key: harness-first, winner in two of three verdicts) and grafts the strongest verified ideas from the other three (learning-first, data-contract-first, deployment-first), closing every fatal flaw a judge listed against any of the four. No training happens in sections 2 to 4. The deck model (section 6) is later work, gated by an owner ruling, not a prerequisite for usable tables.

Six claims stay separate and are never conflated in a report:

1. Representation: a card has a validated program in `data/cards_v1.json` / `card_def.rs` (generated by `build.rs`; module doc confirms `CARD_DEFS` and `card_id_by_name` are both codegen'd from that file, `src/card_def.rs:1-5`).
2. Post-board rule validity: a plan's `cards_in`/`cards_out` conserve the registered 75 and produce a legal 60 plus 15 (`SideboardPlanV1`, `src/sideboard.rs:297`).
3. Playing competence of the current checkpoint on a given 60, measured by the harness (section 2): checkpoint-relative, and for any configuration drawing on a card whose shared embedding-table row is untrained also untrained-embedding-relative. The shared 65,537-entry card-embedding table (`CARD_VOCAB_SIZE_V1`, `src/native_policy_value_net_v1.rs:69`) is indexed by `card_id` across all nine decks' self-play, not per deck: a card_id's row is trained iff that card_id appears in any currently active `RUNTIME_DECKS` mainboard, regardless of which deck's sideboard a given candidate draws it from. Confirmed by direct read: Pulse of Murasa is sideboard-only for Elves in `data/pauper_pool_v1.json` but mainboard for Wildfire, and Wildfire's `materialized_mainboard` in `data/runtime_decks_v1.json` confirms self-play actually plays it, so Pulse of Murasa's row is trained even though it never enters play as an Elves mainboard card; six further cross-deck cases confirmed the same way (Krark-Clan Shaman, Masked Vandal, Dispel, Swamp, End the Festivities, Mesmeric Fiend). A harness number on a 60 that draws a card whose row is untrained by this global, catalog-wide definition measures the checkpoint's competence with a weak representation for that card, not the card's true value; a card that is sideboard-only for the tested deck but mainboard for some other active deck carries no such caveat.
4. Search-table validity: a table is warranted only for the checkpoint that generated it (weights hash plus git HEAD), and only as a measurement of that checkpoint's reproducible preference, not as an absolute card-value ground truth, since claim 3's untrained-embedding caveat applies to every accepted row until section 5's exposure lands (section 4). A new checkpoint invalidates prior tables as the measurement of record; they remain a warm start only.
5. Deck-model competence (section 6): imitation fidelity against the frozen table and RL win rate are different numbers, reported separately, never assumed to agree, once RL refinement is buildable and ruled on at all (section 6, section 10).
6. MTGO transfer: deployment competence on strictly visible-only evidence is measured separately from full-information self-play win rate, and inherits claim 3's embedding caveat.

Existing ratified constraint this design sits inside: Jack's 2026-09-09 sequencing exception (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-09-pauper-meta-cards-and-sideboarding-design.md:149`, "exception granted... nothing measured under this exception is reported as a 'Pauper match' or BO3 promotion result") lets sideboard-plan authoring and Bo3-shaped measurement proceed ahead of the sampled-primary BO1 matrix, but does not on its face cover this document's new search campaign or deck-model claims. Whether it extends to them is an open owner ruling (section 10).

Every receipt (section 4) records which convention produced its hash, sorted-explicit or catalog-materialized (as `hash_convention`), and no identity between an explicit-deck hash and a later `RUNTIME_DECKS` promotion of the same 60 is claimed until `build.rs`'s own codegen check confirms the promoted entry's declared hash matches its materialized hash. Where reconciling the two orderings is impractical, a candidate's identity for search and receipt purposes is its `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id, game_index, cards_in, cards_out)` tuple, not `SessionDeckHashesV1`.

Candidate source: `checked_in_pauper_registered_deck_by_id_v1` plus `RegisteredDeckV1::apply_plan_v1` (`src/sideboard.rs:229, 152`) turn a `SideboardPlanV1` into `DeckConfigurationV1::mainboard()` (`sideboard.rs:68`), a `Vec<u16>`. The harness takes `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id, game_index, cards_in, cards_out)`, materializes the candidate 60 for one seat, and for the other seat resolves the opponent side by `game_index`: at `game_index` 1, the opponent's registered mainboard; at `game_index` 2 or 3, the best BO3-ratified post-board plan already frozen in `data/pauper_sideboard_policy_v1.json` for the `(opponent_deck_id, self_deck_id)` pair if one exists, else the opponent's registered mainboard. This matches `prepare_game_v1`'s bilateral sideboarding for any `game_index` other than 1 (`bo3_session.rs:100-131`, both seats sideboarded via `sideboard_policy.apply_v1` at `bo3_session.rs:119, 124`), which section 4's BO3 ratification stage invokes directly; without this, a BO1 test at `game_index` 2 or 3 would measure a candidate against a configuration production Bo3 never actually presents there. It remains a disclosed simplification, not an exact match to `prepare_game_v1`'s simultaneous within-campaign resolution: an opponent plan accepted mid-campaign is used for every candidate scored after that point, not retroactively rescored against earlier candidates. Each receipt (section 4) records which variant, accepted-opponent-plan or registered-mainboard, was used, and both configurations feed the new resolver. When the accepted-opponent-plan variant is used, the receipt also carries an `opponent_embedding_status` list (section 4), since that plan's `cards_in` draws from the opponent's own registered sideboard zone through the same `apply_plan_v1` mechanism a candidate's does, and so carries the same kind of embedding confound.

Paired seeds: for a fixed opponent, run N paired episodes per candidate against the incumbent plan, reusing the existing `EnvironmentV2` seed pairing (`ResetRandomization::EnvironmentV2 { pair_environment_seed }`, `rl_session.rs`) so candidate and incumbent share identical shuffles and play/draw sequence, canceling shared randomness in the paired difference.

Estimator: per seed, `delta_i = win(candidate) - win(incumbent)` in `{-1, 0, 1}`; report mean delta with a paired-bootstrap confidence interval, matching the existing design's committed unit for match-win claims (`pauper-meta-cards-and-sideboarding-design.md:114`). BO1 games are the unit for search iteration and provisional acceptance (cheap, high iteration count, section 4's first stage); BO3 matches through `BestOfThreeDeckMatchV1::prepare_game_v1` (`src/bo3_session.rs:45, 100`) are the unit for the second-stage ratification measurement (section 4), since game-index 2/3 evaluation needs that path regardless. N (BO1 stage) and M (BO3 stage) are each fixed and pre-registered before any candidate is scored, both recorded in the search-campaign manifest (section 4); no peeking, no early stop on a favorable trend, at either stage.

Cost: one calibration run measures real per-game wall clock under the frozen checkpoint before any campaign-wide N or M is set; this number combines with a pre-registered minimum win-rate delta and target power (section 4) via a stated sample-size formula to set N, not from wall-clock cost alone.

## 3. Game-summary extractor

Source: `state.engine.event_history: Vec<CommittedEvent>` (`src/engine.rs:155`), whose own doc comment calls it the full permanent record of every committed event this game, in commit order, never drained. No variant carries a turn stamp, confirmed across all fourteen variants: `Damage`, `ZoneChange{object, from, to, controller_before}`, `LifeLoss`, `LifeGain`, `Draw{player, object: Option<ObjectId>}`, `Tap`, `ManaAdded`, `CreateToken{object, token_def, controller}`, `SpellCast{spell, controller}`, `Targeted{target, target_zone_change_count, targeting_stack_item, targeting_controller}`, `CombatDamageToPlayer{source, source_zone_change_count, player, amount}`, `Sacrificed{object, controller_before, effective_subtype_ids_before}`, `SagaChapter{source, source_zone_change_count, controller, chapter}`, `OptionalAdditionalCostPaid{source, kind, paid_cost_refs}`, and `InitiativeTrigger{binding}` (`src/event.rs:322-427`). `OptionalAdditionalCostPaid` is never appended to permanent `event_history` per its own doc comment, a transient cast-provenance marker consumed from `event_log` by trigger collection, so the extractor cannot and does not read it from the walked history. The extractor therefore runs in-loop (the same episode driver that steps `RlEpisodeSessionV1`/`FastActorSessionV1`, section 2), watermarking `event_history.len()` against live `GameState.turn` (`src/state.rs:1157`) at each decision boundary, then walks the full history once at episode end using a live `ObjectId -> card_def: u16` map seeded from `GameObject.card_def` (`state.rs:213`) and updated on `CreateToken`/`ZoneChange`.

Fields, per game per side (`GameSummaryV1`):

- `opponent_evidence: Vec<{card_id: u16, first_seen_turn: u16, end_of_game_zone: enum}>`, from the first event revealing an opposing object via a public zone.
- `own_card_outcomes`, keyed by our registered-75 card ids: `{times_drawn: u8, cast: bool, stuck_in_hand: bool, died_without_dealing_damage: bool, removal_no_target: bool, counterspell_held: bool}`. The first four fold from `Draw`, `SpellCast`, end-of-episode hand contents, and a graveyard/exile `ZoneChange` with no preceding `Damage`/`CombatDamageToPlayer` from that incarnation. `removal_no_target` and `counterspell_held` need a second, live signal `event_history` cannot supply after the fact: whether the card was ever a legal cast at all. The extractor also hooks `legal_action_candidates_v5` / `core_policy_action_candidates_v5` (`src/rl.rs:1857, 1882`), the same enumeration the policy already consumes every decision, recording whether a requires-target removal spell in hand was ever offered as a legal cast this game. Combined with the post-hoc "cast but resolved with no traced target" case, this gives an operational definition for both fields instead of a pure post-hoc heuristic. The card-level requires-target/is-counterspell tag set is a small offline metadata file, ratified before the schema freezes (section 10).
- `resource_curve`: `lands_by_turn`, `hand_size_by_turn`, `life_by_turn: [Vec<i16>; 2]`, `first_attack_turn`, `damage_dealt_total`, `damage_taken_total`, folded the same way from `ZoneChange`, `Draw`, `LifeLoss`/`LifeGain`, `CombatDamageToPlayer`, indexed by the turn watermark.
- `schema_version`, `checkpoint_weights_hash`, `checkpoint_git_head`: written at row-creation time, matching what section 4 already writes into the sideboard-policy receipt. `opponent_evidence`, `own_card_outcomes`, and `resource_curve` are checkpoint-relative policy behavior (claim 3, section 1), and section 5 confirms the harness episodes that dominate this store never resolve through the runtime catalog (`resolve_physical_deck_v2`, `native_full_episode_trajectory_v2.rs:548-556`, hard-requires `runtime_deck_by_id` resolution and rejects anything else), so identity is otherwise unrecoverable via trajectory join for exactly the rows that matter most. These three fields make a `GameSummaryV1` row self-describing without that join.

Storage: a new append-only JSONL store, separate from `native_full_episode_trajectory_v2.rs`'s digest-validated training store, one row per `GameSummaryV1`: `{episode_id, deck_ids, deck_hashes, seat, opponent_evidence, own_card_outcomes, resource_curve, winner, schema_version, checkpoint_weights_hash, checkpoint_git_head}`, joined to a trajectory only by `episode_id`. This never touches the training store's provenance digests.

Privacy on MTGO: the schema is shared between two populators. The kernel-native one has full state access but drops anything an MTGO player's own UI would not show (library order, opponent hand). The MTGO populator, built only from `MtgoCompetitiveExternalCompletedMatchHistoryConsumerV1` (trait defined at `integrations/mtgo_dxgi_capture_v1/src/competitive_visible_match_memory.rs:279`, used as a generic bound by `competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs:422` and implemented by `placeholder_sideboard_controller.rs:94`; `competitive_native_sideboard.rs` itself never references the trait, confirmed by a repo-wide grep with zero hits there), is a strict subset by construction, not by a separate filter: every field it can fill is already visible-card-name-keyed and player-visible, matching `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardCardCountV1`'s own doc comment, "Kernel card-database IDs are deliberately absent because they are adapter metadata, not game information shown to the seated player" (`competitive_native_sideboard.rs:18-24`). A field the MTGO stream cannot fill is recorded absent, never inferred.

## 4. Search-generated sideboard tables

Procedure: for each of the 81 ordered `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id)` pairs and each `game_index in {2, 3}`, generate K candidates and score each against the incumbent with the section 2 harness at the pre-registered N. `{2, 3}` is this campaign's scoping choice, not `SideboardPlanV1`'s type domain: `validate_postboard_game_index_v1` (`sideboard.rs:1092-1099`) only rejects `game_index` below 2, so a draw-extended match's fourth or later physical game is a valid postboard `game_index` the type admits but this campaign does not search (`SideboardPlanV1` struct, `sideboard.rs:297-303`). Carry-forward rule: for `game_index` 4 and above, `DeterministicSideboardPolicyV1::plan_for_v1` (`sideboard.rs:521`) falls back to `SideboardDefaultPlanV1::KeepRegisteredConfiguration` when no plan matches the exact `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id, game_index)` triple; this campaign instead reuses the accepted game-3 plan for `game_index` 4 and above, so a draw-extended match still receives the searched configuration rather than silently reverting to the registered 75. This is a known gap against the parent design's section 6.1 ("deliberate behavior for every post-board physical game index"), disclosed pending an owner ruling on its acceptability for v1 (section 10).

Candidate generation: warm-start from `docs/research/sideboard_plan_inputs_2026-09/plan_table_draft_nine_v1.json`, then local search: bounded 1-to-4-card swaps, restricted to cards already present in the deck's own registered 75 so `apply_plan_v1`'s conservation rule is satisfiable by construction. Greedy hill-climbing, incumbent replaced only on acceptance, bounded by a fixed max-iteration count per cell. K, the max-iteration bound, the neighborhood/traversal-order rule, and the warm-start file's hash are fixed numbers in the search-campaign manifest below, not left qualitative.

Search-campaign manifest (file: a new companion manifest file, `sideboard_search_campaign_manifest_v1.json`, committed and hashed alongside the receipts file below, before any candidate is scored): fixes, as concrete values rather than qualitative description, the checkpoint hash and git HEAD (re-verified per cell as the campaign runs, so a mid-campaign checkpoint change aborts rather than silently mixing measurements from two checkpoints); K and the max-iteration bound, as fixed integers; the neighborhood/traversal-order rule, seeded and deterministic; the seed function, a pure function of a master seed, the cell id `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id, game_index)`, and the candidate index, so any candidate's seed list is reproducible from the manifest alone; N per cell and the rule that produced it (section 2's calibration run); M, the BO3 ratification stage's fixed match count; the sha256 of the warm-start plan-inputs file; the exact pre-registered one-sided alpha for the BO1 stage and the fixed confidence level for the BO3 stage, both concrete numbers with no "default" framing; the paired bootstrap's exact resample count, resampling method (case resampling with replacement over the paired per-seed deltas), and sidedness (one-sided for BO1, matching its alpha; two-sided for BO3's CI-excludes-0 test); and N's derivation, computed not from wall-clock cost alone but from a pre-registered minimum win-rate delta (the smallest effect worth detecting) and a target power, combined with the section 2 calibration run's real per-game cost through a stated sample-size formula recorded in the manifest, so N is a power calculation, not a budget-driven guess. No candidate scores before this manifest is committed and hashed; a checkpoint mismatch detected at any per-cell re-verification aborts the run rather than continuing under an unrecorded checkpoint. Ratifying this manifest, alongside the acceptance rule and N, is an owner ruling (section 10).

Acceptance rule, pre-registered before search starts, two stages: (1) BO1 provisional accept: a candidate replaces the incumbent as the search's working best only if the paired-bootstrap CI lower bound on mean BO1 delta exceeds 0 at the manifest's exact pre-registered one-sided alpha (a fixed number fixed in the manifest before scoring starts, not a default), using the full pre-declared N once, no repeated testing at partial N, no stopping early on a trend. (2) BO3 ratification: only a cell's final provisionally accepted candidate advances to a pre-registered BO3 pass, M matches (fixed, pre-registered, section 10) through `BestOfThreeDeckMatchV1::prepare_game_v1` (`bo3_session.rs:100`), using the section 2 harness's opponent-side resolution rule, evaluated at the manifest's fixed two-sided confidence level. Both stages' bootstrap CIs use the manifest's pre-registered resample count, resampling method, and sidedness (section 4's manifest fields above: one-sided for BO1, two-sided for BO3), never an implicit or ad hoc choice at analysis time. Only a candidate whose BO3 paired match-win-delta CI also excludes 0 at that confidence level is written into `data/pauper_sideboard_policy_v1.json`; a candidate that clears BO1 but fails BO3 ratification is logged, with both CIs, and the incumbent is retained. Only BO3-ratified rows are MTGO-eligible (section 6). This closes the gap where a BO1-only accepted plan could otherwise be written into the deployed policy file without the BO3 path section 2 names ever being invoked.

Freezing and disclosure (file: a new companion receipts file alongside `data/pauper_sideboard_policy_v1.json`): every evaluated candidate, accepted or rejected, is logged with its `cards_in`/`cards_out`, a `hash_convention` field recording which convention (`sorted-explicit` or `catalog-materialized`, section 2) produced this candidate's identity hash, an `embedding_status` list (one entry per `card_id` in `cards_in`, each marked `trained` iff that card_id appears in any currently active `RUNTIME_DECKS` mainboard, checked against the live catalog at receipt-write time, `untrained` otherwise, per claim 3's global, catalog-wide definition, section 1, not the tested deck's own mainboard), the BO1 stage's measured delta/CI/N/seed list, the BO3 stage's measured delta/CI/M/seed list when the candidate reached that stage, which opponent-side resolution variant (section 2) was used, and, whenever the accepted-opponent-plan variant was used, an `opponent_embedding_status` list computed the same way as `embedding_status` (one entry per card_id the opponent's accepted plan draws into its mainboard via `apply_plan_v1`, `sideboard.rs:152-183`, marked trained/untrained against the live `RUNTIME_DECKS` catalog at receipt-write time), so a rejected candidate stays auditable and rerunnable, not only the winners, and a game_index-2/3 measurement using an accepted opponent plan discloses its own embedding confound on the opponent side, not only the candidate's. `apply_plan_v1` draws `cards_in` from the tested deck's own registered sideboard zone (`sideboard.rs:152-183`), but that zone is not uniformly untrained: a card sideboard-only for the tested deck can be mainboard, and therefore self-play-exposed and trained, for another active deck (claim 3, section 1). Each candidate's `embedding_status` list, checked against the live catalog at receipt-write time, is therefore the operative record, not an assumption that every `cards_in` row is untrained by construction; only cards whose id appears in no active `RUNTIME_DECKS` mainboard carry the untrained-embedding caveat forward into `data/pauper_sideboard_policy_v1.json`. Only a BO3-ratified plan's row plus the exact checkpoint identity (weights hash, git HEAD) is written into `data/pauper_sideboard_policy_v1.json`, committed off the launch branch, carrying the same untrained-embedding qualification forward into the policy file itself so no accepted row is described there as unqualified "ground truth." From that point the plan is a pre-registered constant: no further outcome-based tuning without a new registration and a fresh search.

Regeneration per checkpoint: a table is valid only for the checkpoint recorded in its receipt (claim 4, section 1). A new checkpoint invalidates prior tables as measurement; every prior table is retained on disk by checkpoint hash for audit, never overwritten, and remains usable only as the next search's warm start. The search-campaign manifest's per-cell checkpoint re-verification (above) is the guard against a mid-campaign checkpoint change going undetected.

## 5. Training exposure

Mechanism only, not a scheduling decision. Today `RUNTIME_DECKS` (`src/runtime_decks.rs:11`) is generated by `build.rs` from `data/runtime_decks_v1.json`; sideboards in `data/pauper_pool_v1.json` are registered but never played. To expose a BO3-ratified post-board 60 (section 4's output) to self-play, add it as a named entry (file: `data/runtime_decks_v1.json`). This is not a catalog-rows-only change: `build.rs` fixes `const EXPECTED_DECKS: [(&str, u32, &str, &str, usize, u64); 9]` (`build.rs:1955`) as a literal nine-element tuple array and panics if `catalog.decks.len() != EXPECTED_DECKS.len()` or if any deck's id, canonical pool order, source path, or source sha256 does not positionally match its frozen tuple (`build.rs:2067-2100`, verified by direct read). Every promotion therefore requires a `build.rs` edit: a new literal row appended to `EXPECTED_DECKS`, bumping the fixed array length by one (9 to 10, then 10 to 11, and so on per promotion). A promoted post-board deck also has no real `.dek` source file behind it, so the fields that `build.rs` otherwise derives from a vendored XMage file, `source_path`/`source_sha256` and the per-copy `materialized_mainboard: Vec<RuntimeDeckCopyJson>` rows (`build.rs:132, 137-139`, each a `{source_row_ordinal, copy_ordinal}` pair that `build.rs:2124-2164` validates for strict per-row-then-per-copy ordering), must be synthesized rather than read off a real file: the promotion tooling assigns a documented synthetic source path and hash, and walks the accepted candidate's 60-card multiset in a fixed, disclosed order to build a `materialized_mainboard` row structure whose ordinals satisfy the same validation `build.rs` already runs against real decks, so the codegen check passes without a real `.dek` behind the promoted entry. This is a real, disclosed engineering cost, not "no code change, only more catalog rows." `resolve_runtime_decks` (`rl_session.rs:6992`) itself needs no change; only `build.rs`'s hardcoded catalog contract does, on every promotion. The catalog id is not the bare deck name: it embeds the accepted table's checkpoint identity and a search-generation ordinal, for example `Rally_pb_g2_vs_Wildfire_ck<weights-hash-prefix>_gen1`, so a re-search of the same `(self_deck_id, opponent_deck_id, game_index)` cell that accepts a different candidate always produces a new id and never overwrites or silently redefines a live catalog entry another `RUNTIME_DECKS` consumer already references. Ratifying this versioning convention before the first promotion is an owner ruling (section 10). `SessionDeckIdsV1` and the session contract are unchanged; `flat_policy_v2.rs` and `CommittedEvent` stay untouched.

This is purely additive on the training-store side too: `native_full_episode_trajectory_v2.rs`'s admission gate, `resolve_physical_deck_v2` (line 548), hard-requires `runtime_deck_by_id` resolution and rejects anything else as `InvalidDeckId` (line 553). A catalog-promoted post-board 60 satisfies this unmodified, no edit to the provenance gate itself; section 2's harness episodes, which never resolve through the catalog, correctly never enter this store at all.

Science-lane boundary: whether and when to spend a training cycle on post-board exposure, and the self-play sampling mix across mainboard/post-board pairings, is an owner ruling (section 10).

## 6. Deck model v1

Inputs: our registered 75 (card ids plus per-card outcome features from section 3), the opponent-evidence set from recent `GameSummaryV1` rows against this opponent (or Unknown), match score (`next_game_number`, `acting_player_games_won`, `opponent_games_won`), current configuration.

Architecture: the shared card-embedding table (`CARD_VOCAB_SIZE_V1 = 65,537` rows by `CARD_EMBEDDING_DIM_V1 = 16`, `src/native_policy_value_net_v1.rs:68-69`) feeds two permutation-invariant set encoders (masked mean or small attention pooling): one over the 75 registered cards, one over the variable-length opponent-evidence tuples, with an explicit Unknown-card bucket for MTGO's hidden identity. Within a Bo3 match, the 0-to-2 completed games are pooled with an explicit game-index positional feature rather than plain permutation-invariant pooling, since which game a piece of evidence came from matters: game-1 evidence predates any sideboarding, game-2 evidence postdates it. The embedding table is loaded read-only from the frozen play-model checkpoint: the deck model never writes to it, so its card representation cannot drift from the play model's own, and the two cannot co-adapt during RL refinement below. Pooled outputs concatenate with resource-curve summary statistics into one match-context vector feeding policy and value heads.

Action space, internal representation: sequential swaps keyed by card_id, with a done action, matching the vocabulary already implemented for MTGO, `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardDeliberationActionV1` (`integrations/mtgo_dxgi_capture_v1/src/competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs:29-32`), bounded by `MTGO_COMPETITIVE_NATIVE_SIDEBOARD_MAX_DECISIONS_V1 = 64` (same file, line 15). That enum's real fields are `MoveOneToMainboard{visible_card_name: String}` / `MoveOneToSideboard{visible_card_name: String}`, deliberately name-keyed, not id-keyed: its own doc comment states "no adapter card ID... is present" (lines 26-28), matching the Daybreak condition. The deck model's policy head therefore emits card_id logits internally, and only the MTGO slot implementation translates at the boundary, reusing code that already exists for exactly this purpose elsewhere in the MTGO integration: `card_id_by_name` (generated by `build.rs:6495` from `data/cards_v1.json`, confirmed already called at `integrations/mtgo_dxgi_capture_v1/src/actuator.rs:17827` and `competitive_auxiliary_action_resolution.rs:485`) resolves an incoming visible name to a card_id, and the reverse lookup for emitting an action is `CARD_DEFS[card_id].name` (`src/card_def.rs:934`, `CardDef.name: &'static str`). No new translation code is invented, only a call site added at the slot boundary; this closes the "no translation layer" gap a judge found in an earlier draft.

Imitation target: behavior-clone against the frozen search tables (section 4), converting each accepted plan's `cards_in`/`cards_out` into a canonical swap sequence. `canonicalize_card_counts_v1` (`sideboard.rs:1102-1117`) already sorts both `cards_in` and `cards_out` by `card_id`, but does not by itself state how the two lists interleave, or how a count-greater-than-one row expands into sequential actions; two implementations could otherwise produce different clone targets for the same plan. Canonical rule: emit all `cards_out` rows first in ascending `card_id` order (each row's count expanded into that many individual sideboard-bound swap actions), then all `cards_in` rows in ascending `card_id` order (each expanded the same way), then the terminal done action, mirroring the existing kind-grouped, terminal-action-last discipline already documented on `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardDeliberationDecisionV1`'s `ordered_actions` field ("Actions are ordered first by action kind, then by canonical visible card name, with SubmitConfiguration last," `integrations/mtgo_dxgi_capture_v1/src/competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs`). The measured win-rate delta from the plan's BO3 ratification stage (section 4) is the value-head target.

RL refinement (blocked on W8a, section 8; gated by its own training-cycle ruling, section 10): after imitation warm-start, the design intends to refine via Bo3 self-play with terminal match win-loss as the sole reward, matching the existing design's reserved-slot framing (`pauper-meta-cards-and-sideboarding-design.md:116`), KL-regularized toward the imitation policy to bound drift off the audited table's coverage. The play-model checkpoint stays frozen throughout, for the same no-co-adaptation reason as the embedding table above. This is presently unbuildable, not merely unscheduled: `prepare_game_v1` (`bo3_session.rs:100-135`) hardcodes post-board sideboarding to `sideboard_policy.apply_v1` for both seats (calls at `bo3_session.rs:119, 124`) with no override point, and neither `rl.rs` nor `rl_session.rs` contains any BO3 or sideboard machinery, so no path today connects a live swap policy to a real Bo3 match. W8a (section 8) is the blocking prerequisite: a `BestOfThreeDeckModelMatchV1` variant of `bo3_session.rs`, or a new `prepare_game_v1` parameter, accepting a live per-seat swap sequence in place of `sideboard_policy.apply_v1`, plus a self-play driver using `MatchOutcomeV1::Winner` (`bo3_match.rs:29`) as reward. Its self-play driver constructs every simulated game from `PreparedMatchGameV1::start().starting_player` (section 2), via the section 2 starting-player-aware explicit-deck constructor, never the plain P0-only one; a self-play driver that ignored the BO3-assigned starting player would corrupt the reward signal's play/draw split the same way an unfixed W6 would have corrupted the ratification measurement. Until W8a lands, only imitation-warm-start can be scored by the section 2 harness; RL refinement is blocked, not reserved. Separately, whether and when to spend a training cycle on RL refinement at all is its own science-lane owner ruling (section 10), parallel to section 5's post-board-exposure ruling and prior to the reward-shape ruling below: until both W8a lands and that ruling is granted, W8 (section 8) ships imitation-only.

Evaluation: identical harness and two-stage single-shot acceptance discipline as sections 2 and 4 (BO1 provisional, BO3 ratification), including the manifest's exact pre-registered alpha, confidence level, and bootstrap resample specification (section 4), not only the two-stage structure; a promoted model version is itself a pre-registered constant with the same freeze and disclosure. Unlike the archetype classifier it replaces, the deck model's action space has no discrete Unknown/abstain action, so "submit unchanged under low-confidence evidence" (the placeholder's fail-closed default, carried forward below) is emergent behavior, not a tested branch. Before promotion: run the deck model offline against visible-evidence prefixes from outside-pool opponent decks (the same held-out-decks discipline the parent design's section 6.2 already uses for the classifier) and report the rate and character of non-trivial swaps it makes under sparse or out-of-pool evidence as a disclosed diagnostic. This is a diagnostic, not folded into the acceptance gate: it carries no numeric promotion threshold of its own, but a promotion whose diagnostic shows frequent non-trivial swaps on sparse or unrecognized evidence is disclosed as such alongside the promotion.

MTGO deployment: implements `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardScorerV1` and `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardDeliberationScorerV1` behind the existing slot, replacing `MtgoPlaceholderSideboardControllerV1` (`placeholder_sideboard_controller.rs`), consuming completed-match history via `MtgoCompetitiveExternalCompletedMatchHistoryConsumerV1` (trait at `competitive_visible_match_memory.rs:279`). Because `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardModelInputV1` carries no opponent-deck-id field at all (confirmed at `competitive_native_sideboard.rs:39-43`: `next_game_number`, `acting_player_games_won`, `opponent_games_won`, `current_configuration` only), the deck model cannot and does not take a classifier-resolved opponent id as input; it conditions on visible evidence directly, so opponent-id inference and plan lookup collapse into one learned function, subsuming the archetype classifier from the existing design's sections 6.2-6.3 without also depending on that classifier's output.

Unknown floor (file: a new slot-binder layer in `integrations/mtgo_dxgi_capture_v1/src/`, wrapping `advance_competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation_v1`): the parent design's deterministic Unknown bypass has no analog once a learned policy replaces the placeholder. `advance_competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation_v1` (`competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs:307-314`) always calls `scorer.score_sideboard_deliberation_v1` unconditionally, with no Unknown short-circuit inside the deliberation loop itself, and self-play always knows the true opponent, so Unknown-evidence exposure during training may be far sparser than MTGO's actual rate of unrecognized or ambiguous opponents. The fix is not inside the trained policy: the MTGO slot binder, sitting above the deliberation loop, enforces Unknown-preserves-current directly. When opponent evidence resolves to Unknown or below a pre-registered confidence floor, the binder short-circuits to `SubmitConfiguration` (the placeholder's fail-closed default) without ever invoking the scorer, so the untested emergent-abstention risk noted in Evaluation above is bypassed at the boundary rather than trusted to the policy. This floor is a pre-registered constant needing its own owner ruling before `qualified_for_live` (section 10), matching the parent design's still-open ruling 4 (`pauper-meta-cards-and-sideboarding-design.md` section 9 item 4, the Unknown-preserves-current confirmation).

Audit receipt (file: the same slot-binder layer, alongside `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardDeliberationStepReceiptV1` in `competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs`): the parent design's 6.3 requires receipts carrying classifier version, per-deck scores, abstention reason, and selected plan id, but the verified response and receipt types carry none of these (`MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardScoreResponseV1`, `competitive_auxiliary_model_scoring.rs:266-271`, has only `schema_version`, `model_input_commitment_sha256`, `deployment_commitment_sha256`, `selection`, `value_f32_bits`; `MtgoCompetitiveNativeSideboardDeliberationStepReceiptV1`, `competitive_native_sideboard_deliberation.rs:75-86`, carries no classifier or plan identity either), so "no new translation code" above does not by itself satisfy 6.3. The slot binder writes a separate audit receipt per response, parallel to but not replacing the verified step receipt: checkpoint identity (weights hash, git HEAD) stands in for classifier version, an opponent-evidence summary (or an explicit Unknown marker) stands in for per-deck scores, the Unknown/low-evidence-floor trigger (or none) is the abstention reason, and the nearest frozen table entry id, or an explicit not-a-table-lookup marker when the policy's swap sequence does not correspond to any single frozen row, is the selected plan id. This audit-receipt shape is ratified together with the deck model's promotion (section 10).

## 7. Brewing extension

Reused: the set encoder, the shared embedding table, the sequential-swap action framing (generalizes from a closed 75 to an open pool), the paired harness and evaluation protocol.

Must change: the action space widens from the registered 75 to the full pool (150 non-token cards today) and eventually beyond it. The fixed embedding table stops being sufficient once brewing must reason about cards with no self-play exposure; the untrained rows among the 15-card sideboards, that is, the cards whose card_id appears in no active `RUNTIME_DECKS` mainboard (claim 3, section 1), are the same failure mode at smaller scale.

Two independent prerequisite gates, not one:

(a) Representation gate: a feature- or text-based card encoder (mana cost, types, oracle-text embedding) replacing or augmenting id lookup, so an unseen card id yields a usable vector instead of an untrained row. This is a distinct, larger workstream, explicitly out of scope for v1, matching the owner's stated build order.

(b) Engine-completeness gate: `preflight_fully_supported_deck` (`card_def.rs:1344`) hard-rejects any deck containing a card whose `CardCapability` (`card_def.rs:68-77`) is `Partial` or `NoEffect`, unconditionally, at construction time. Brewing an unregistered configuration means feeding `preflight_fully_supported_deck` a deck the registered-75 waves never assembled, so every brewing-candidate card needs a complete `CardCapability::Full` engine program, not just a registry entry. This is the larger, per-card effort already priced in the sibling design's W2-W6 (`pauper-meta-cards-and-sideboarding-design.md` section 7, rows 5-7), not a brewing-specific cost, but brewing cannot exceed the currently-implemented, Full-capability card set until that effort is funded, independent of and in addition to gate (a).

## 9. Risks

`event_history` has no turn stamp, coupling the extractor to the self-play driver rather than an offline replay tool (mitigated: `flat_policy_v2.rs` and `CommittedEvent` stay untouched). Search cost scales as N times 81 pairs times up to 2 game indices times K candidates, now compounded by the BO3 ratification stage's M matches per provisionally accepted candidate; under-provisioning N or M inflates false-accept risk on the pre-registered CI rules, over-provisioning burns compute budget, so W2's calibration must land before committing to a campaign-wide N and M, and N itself is now a power calculation against a pre-registered minimum win-rate delta (section 4), not a cost-driven guess, so the delta and power target need their own care at ratification time. Without the committed, hashed search-campaign manifest (section 4), a mid-campaign checkpoint change could go undetected; the manifest's per-cell checkpoint re-verification is the only guard, and running the campaign before it is hashed is disqualifying, not merely risky. The `game_index` 4-and-above carry-forward rule (section 4) is a disclosed gap against the parent design's section 6.1 until an owner rules on it: a draw-extended match's real fourth game may warrant a plan the accepted game-3 plan does not capture. RL refinement (section 6) has no BO3-connected match path today; W8a is a blocking engineering prerequisite and its training-cycle ruling is a separate gate, so any schedule assuming RL refinement ships alongside W8's imitation warm-start is wrong until both clear. MTGO opponent evidence per League match is sparse, so the deployed model may see far fewer evidence tokens than self-play summaries provide; the slot binder's Unknown/low-confidence floor (section 6) is the enforced degrade path, not an emergent property of the encoder, but self-play never exercises that binder because self-play always knows the true opponent, so the floor's behavior is validated only by the out-of-pool holdout diagnostic (section 6) and the binder's own tests, never by training signal. `removal_no_target` and `counterspell_held` now have an operational definition (section 3) but it still rests on a hand-authored requires-target/is-counterspell tag file; an unreviewed or drifted tag silently corrupts the field with no failing test. Terminal match win-loss is a single bit spread over a multi-hundred-decision RL episode; if this proves too sparse in practice, a batch-averaged harness win-rate delta over several matches is the fallback, but that is a deviation from the existing design's section 6.5 framing and needs its own owner ruling before use, not a silent substitution, and now sits behind the RL training-cycle ruling above rather than in front of it. The MTGO card_id/visible_card_name translation (section 6) depends on exact string matches between `CARD_DEFS[id].name` and the adapter's captured name; client-side aliasing, localization, or punctuation drift would silently break the mapping and needs its own validation pass before the slot goes live. Post-board catalog entries touch `build.rs` (section 5, W7: a new `EXPECTED_DECKS` literal row and a synthesized `materialized_mainboard` row/ordinal structure per promotion, not a catalog-only edit) and must be sequenced outside any launch or build freeze window.
